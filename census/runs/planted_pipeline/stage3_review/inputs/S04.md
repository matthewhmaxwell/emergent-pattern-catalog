**Verdict: KNOWN.** This behaviour is already documented. My one caveat is that I found no paper using this exact nutrient-plus-attractant setup with this sensitivity function in 2D and showing the same moving patterns.

**Why the nutrient acts as a repellent.** Cells eat the nutrient where they are dense, and it is resupplied everywhere at a slow rate (0.084). So for small changes, nutrient level ≈ 1 minus a smoothed copy of cell density. Moving up the nutrient gradient is therefore the same as moving away from a slow, long-range signal that the cells themselves make. That makes the system a standard attraction-repulsion Keller-Segel model:
- **Attractant:** short range (√2.5 ≈ 1.6 length units) and fast (turns over at rate 1).
- **Effective repellent (nutrient shortage):** longer range (√(0.75/0.084) ≈ 3) and about 12 times slower.

Short-range attraction combined with slower, longer-range repulsion is the textbook recipe for patterns that keep changing over time instead of settling down.

**How the sub-programs match the literature:**
- **Attractant only (rule 1):** strong clumps that barely change (f_std 2.46, f_change 0.01), meaning a frozen or slowly coarsening set of clumps. This is the documented case without growth, where multiple-peak patterns are unstable and a coarsening process forms that leads (over long time scales) to a unique global aggregation.
- **Nutrient only (rule 2):** stays uniform. Repulsion alone just smooths things out, which is expected.
- **Both together:** clumps that keep changing (f_change 0.35). This is the documented attraction-repulsion result. Liu, Shi and Wang prove that the attraction-repulsion Keller-Segel system has both time-periodic and steady patterns, and argue that chemotactic competitive interaction between attraction and repulsion can produce periodic patterns which are impossible for the chemotaxis model with a single chemical. Painter's review of dual-signal models also reports temporal patterns (nonuniform solutions undergoing sustained temporal dynamics).

**Same model in biological form.** Bacterial models that combine self-secreted attractant with taxis toward a nutrient the cells consume are well established. In one, the motion of the swarm ring is driven by local nutrient depletion, with the integrity resulting from the high concentration of the attractant. A simulation study of E. coli shows dynamical patterns formed by a bacterial colony which chemotactically senses gradients in both chemoattractant and nutrient concentrations.

**Related continually changing clump dynamics.** Painter and Hillen show models with a volume-filling sensitivity that undergo a sustained spatio-temporal sequence of merging (two aggregations coalesce) and emerging (a new aggregation appears). There, cell growth does the job that the nutrient-shortage repulsion does here. The sensitivity used here, b/(1+b²), is another crowding-limited form, so it is not a meaningful difference in behaviour.
