**Verdict: KNOWN-VARIANT, low confidence.** I found no paper on this exact rule, and I could not check the simulation's numbers against any source.

**1. Model family.** This is a stochastic mass-transport process (MTP) on a periodic square lattice. Mass is continuous, the dynamics are random-sequential, and total mass is conserved. Chirality is added because the second hop always turns left. That is an odd, parity-breaking but isotropic-in-rate rule, so detailed balance is broken. The closest literatures are:
- mass-transport processes and generalized zero-range processes;
- hyperuniformity in non-equilibrium conserved systems;
- chiral and odd-diffusion lattice models.

**2. What the literature says about this behaviour.**
- **Generic MTPs.** The mass-transport process is defined and its product-measure steady states are characterised in Greenblatt & Lebowitz, J. Phys. A, "Product measure steady states of generalized zero range processes". The search result shows the process and the factorizability condition. I did not see the year or full author list, so those are unverified. If a rule like this one has a factorised steady state, density correlations would be short-ranged and S(k→0) would be finite, with no hyperuniformity. I did not test whether this rule satisfies the factorizability condition.
- **Hyperuniformity needs a center-of-mass conservation law.** Hazra, Mukherjee & Pradhan, J. Stat. Mech. (2025) 023201, "Hyperuniformity in mass transport processes with center-of-mass conservation: some exact results", treat 1D and 2D MTPs with broken detailed balance. They find dynamic and static fluctuations are more suppressed in systems with CoMC, resulting in an extreme ('class-I') hyperuniformity in certain cases. They also give S(q) ∼ q², implying a class-I spatial hyperuniformity.
- **Review-level source.** Maire et al., J. Chem. Phys. 163, 214507 (2025), "Hyperuniformity and conservation laws in non-equilibrium systems" (arXiv:2509.04242), argue that hyperuniformity emerges generically from the interplay between conservation laws and non-equilibrium driving. With m conserved multipoles, S(k) ∼ k^m. They also report that hyperuniformity arising from conserved noise and partially conserved average motion is not robust against non-linear perturbations.
- **Chirality alone.** Chiral and odd-diffusion lattice models exist, for example Wójcik & Kalz, New J. Phys., "The chiral random walk: a quantum-inspired framework for odd diffusion". Odd diffusivity is reported to leave the collective diffusion coefficient unchanged: the collective diffusion coefficient is unaffected from odd diffusion. Chiral circle-swimmer fluids show hyperuniformity, for example the Science Advances paper "Nonequilibrium strongly hyperuniform fluids of circle active particles with large local density fluctuations", but there the mechanism is center-of-mass-conserving collisions plus Fickian diffusion.

**3. Fit to this rule.** For one move, the center of mass shifts by a·d + b·l(d). Here a is the mass sent in the first hop, b the mass passed on in the second, d the chosen direction and l(d) its left turn. This is not zero event by event. It averages to zero only over the four directions. So the noise is not dipole-conserving, and the Hazra et al. and Maire et al. mechanism for hyperuniformity does not apply. I expect an ordinary non-hyperuniform MTP with finite long-wavelength fluctuations and some short-range structure. That fits the reported spatial structure (Moran 0.10, about 6 blobs, a finite-k peak at 0.417). Chirality is probably a minor modification, since it only adds odd (antisymmetric) transport terms. A rule with exactly this two-step left-turn structure is not described in anything I found.

**What differs from the closest sources, and whether it matters.** The closest sources are CoM-conserving MTPs and chiral random walks. Neither has the two-hop, always-left-turning mass-sharing move. I judge that it probably does not matter for the main result, which is non-hyperuniform fluctuations, but I have not tested that.

**Confidence: low.** Only 7 searches were run, and I found no paper that simulates this exact rule. I did not check the Hazra et al. or Maire et al. text for a model close to this one. I also did not compute the structure factor myself.
