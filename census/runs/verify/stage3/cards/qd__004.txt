**What the metrics show.** One type is left (n_types=1), it fills almost the whole grid (largest_domain≈0.999), and activity rises briefly and then falls to zero (strongly negative trend, high variability). The correlation length is maxed out, and there is one reversal. Together this says the grid ends up all one type and freezes. In a uniform grid, correlation length and domain size hit their maximum automatically, so those two numbers don't add independent evidence of structure.

**Why this is already documented:**
1. **Payoff-based plus conformity-based updating on a lattice freezing into one type.** Lin et al. (2020) combine the two update rules on a lattice and report that the system "is always absorbed into the frozen state". Three phases are appeared in the order of full cooperators, bistability and full defectors with increasing temptation to defect *b*. Their "bistability" phase means the system ends all-cooperator or all-defector depending on the run, which fits a single reversal and a random winner.
2. **The same thing with these snowdrift payoffs.** Szolnoki & Perc mapped the full temptation–sucker's-payoff plane (T, S) for the snowdrift game with both learning rules competing.
