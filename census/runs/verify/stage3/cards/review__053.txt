**What the rules force (my own reasoning; I can't see your time series):**
- Rule (2) runs every step, for every node, on every link to a different type. So after rule (1) fires once, every link between two different types is replaced by a link to a same-type node.
- After that one pass, every link joins two nodes of the same type. Rule (1) needs a neighbour of type t+1, so it can never fire again. Rule (2) has nothing left to cut. The system freezes within about one to two steps.
- Your features follow from that:
  - **Fragmentation and many components:** the network splits into at most 4 single-type groups, plus any nodes left with no links.
  - **Large giant component:** that is just the most common type.
  - **High edge turnover:** in a random start, roughly 3/4 of links join different types, and all of them get rewired in one go.
  - **Higher degree spread:** rewiring sends links to random targets and strips links from the nodes left behind.
  - **Modest consensus gain:** a single update of the cyclic rule shifts the type counts a little, then everything freezes.
