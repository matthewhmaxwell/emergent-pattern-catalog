**1. The game is a textbook case.** Because S > P (0.5 > 0), this is a snowdrift game, not a Prisoner's Dilemma. Using the Hauert–Doebeli payoff form (R = b−c/2, S = b−c, T = b, P = 0), your numbers mean b = 1.5 and c = 1, so their cost-to-benefit ratio r = c/(2b−c) is exactly 0.5. They studied this setup on lattices and found that in unstructured snowdrift games, intermediate levels of cooperation persist. Unexpectedly, spatial structure reduces the proportion of cooperators for a wide range of parameters. The reason is that it is advantageous to adopt strategies that are opposite to neighboring strategies. That explains the uneven patches of the two types and the early chaotic churn your detector flagged as "spatial game chaos".

**2. The noise step is a textbook voter model.** Copying a random neighbour's type is the voter model. On a 2D lattice it approaches one of the two absorbing states with complete consensus via a coarsening process, and does so slowly: the density of active interfaces vanishes only as 1/ln(t/τ).
