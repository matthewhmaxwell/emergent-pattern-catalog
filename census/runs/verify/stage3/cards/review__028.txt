**Why the first pass's framing doesn't hold**
- The documented Schelling–Voter hybrid works differently. There, an unhappy agent is selected at random, and she chooses to play one of two different games: either she migrates to another place where she will be happy (with probability p), or she changes her own type (with probability 1−p). In this system, every cell copies a random neighbour every step, whether or not it is unhappy.
- That makes rule (2), applied to all cells including empty ones, a synchronous voter model with three states, where "empty" is one of the states. This is my reading of the rule text, not something I measured. If it's right, vacancies are not conserved, so the vacancy mechanism behind Schelling coarsening doesn't apply as such.
- The copying rule is strong enough to swamp the move rule. That explains why 33 rule programs with thresholds from 0.2 to 0.4, either rule order and 3–4 types all give the same fingerprint. The threshold and the order barely matter.

**Why the observed fingerprint matches voter coarsening**
- The 2D voter model is a known marginal case. It coarsens through interfacial noise, not curvature. It is characterized by a slow, logarithmic decay of the density of interfaces ρ∼1/
