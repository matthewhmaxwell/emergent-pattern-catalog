First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** The system is a standard adaptive (coevolving) voter model, with a few implementation differences that probably don't change the outcome.

**1. Model family.** This is the adaptive or coevolving voter model on a random graph. Nodes copy a random neighbour's state (voter dynamics). Nodes that sit on a discordant link cut it and rewire to a random same-state node ("rewire-to-same"). The model has 3 states, an Erdős–Rényi network with mean degree about 4, and a low rewiring probability (0.1) compared with copying (1.0).

**2. Earliest clear sources for this behaviour.**
- **Holme & Newman (2006), Phys. Rev. E 74, 056108.**
  - This is the earliest clear match. Their model has G opinions and rewiring to same-opinion nodes.
  - The model reaches a consensus state in finite time on a finite network.
  - The structure of the consensus state shows clear signatures of a continuous phase transition as the balance between opinion change and rewiring is varied.
  - Related work (Durrett et al.) describes the dynamics ending when no edges join different opinions. In that setting the end state is either a single connected consensus or several clusters, each in internal consensus (Kozma & Barrat, in the bounded-confidence version).
