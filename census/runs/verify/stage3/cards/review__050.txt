First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** The first pass got the mechanism wrong. This isn't spirals broken up by moving agents. It's the textbook low-threshold phase of the cyclic cellular automaton, where cells oscillate locally without forming waves. The segregation (moving) rule adds little on top of that.

**Why the first pass was wrong**

Rule (1) on its own is Griffeath's cyclic cellular automaton (CCA): 4 states, 8 neighbours, threshold 1. The empty state is just one of the 4 colours in the cycle. That exact setting is already described in the literature, and it does not produce spirals:
- One paper used exactly this setup for its figures: the number of colors is fixed to 4, the rule uses a range-one box-shaped neighborhood, and the initial conditions are a uniform random mixture of colors.
- For that neighbourhood, T=1 and T=2 are both locally periodic, but T=2 produces spiral waves, while T=1 "quenches" incoherent local oscillations.
- What it looks like: a slight departure from uniform randomness, in the shape of connected twisting single-color bands, and the CA as a whole oscillates with period 4, each cell cycling through the colors.
- More generally, a disordered soup virtually indistinguishable from noise results if the threshold is too low. Spirals only appear at intermediate thresholds.
