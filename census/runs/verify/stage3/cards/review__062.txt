**What the rule is**
- Without the swap, rule (1) is the "313" cyclic cellular automaton: range 1, threshold 3, 3 states, 8 neighbours. The only differences are that it updates at random (p=0.3 or 0.5) instead of all at once, and it runs on a 64×64 wrap-around grid. In the standard catalogue, 313 is "the only three-color rule in the range-1 CCA rulespace that exhibits significant self-organization", and it sports a number of distinct spiral types. The general rule family is set out in Fisch, Gravner and Griffeath: colors advance by contact with at least a threshold number of successor colors in a prescribed local neighborhood.
- Adding neighbour swaps makes the cells mobile, which turns this into spatial rock-paper-scissors with mobility. Reichenbach, Mobilia and Frey (Nature 2007) found a critical threshold of motility above which biodiversity is lost, but below which an entanglement of travelling spiral waves emerges and biodiversity is maintained. They state this is robust and does not depend on the details of cyclic competition or spatial environment, so a threshold-based cyclic update is covered by the same picture.
