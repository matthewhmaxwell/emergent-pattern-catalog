**1. This exact rule is a well-known, named rule.** Three colours, Moore neighbourhood, range 1, threshold 3 is "313" (R1/T3/C3/NM) in Mirek Wójtowicz's MCell catalogue. It is credited to David Griffeath and described as "the only three-color rule in the range-1 CCA rulespace that exhibits significant self-organization." It sports a number of distinct spiral types. So the textbook behaviour for this rule is spiral waves, not coarsening.

**2. The first pass picked the wrong reference phase.** The metastable "turbulent" phase it cited comes from Shalizi et al., whose figures use 4 colours. In that setting, at T=3 one finds "turbulence," which is metastable: spiral waves can form and take over the whole grid, but on finite lattices this doesn't always happen, and the turbulent phase can last a very long time. That describes the 4-colour rule (R1/T3/C4), not this 3-colour one. The overall phase picture still applies, though: at high thresholds you get static solid blocks (fixation); at intermediate thresholds, wave fronts form, travel for a while, then break up.
