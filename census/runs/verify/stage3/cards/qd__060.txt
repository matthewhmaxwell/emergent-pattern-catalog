**Why this is a known system**
- **The cell layer is Nowak–May.** Each cell plays itself and its 8 neighbours, all cells update at once, and each copies its best-scoring neighbour. That is exactly the setup in which C- and D-players coexist, with clusters expanding, colliding and fragmenting. Nowak and May observed that the deterministic "imitate-the-best" update rule generates chaotic dynamics.
- **The payoffs make it a snowdrift game, not a Prisoner's Dilemma.** T=1.5 > R=1 > S=0.5 > P=0 is the snowdrift ordering, with b=1.5, c=1, giving a cost-to-benefit ratio of 0.5. That changes the patterns in a known way: in the spatial snowdrift game cooperators form only small filament-like clusters, and it becomes advantageous to adopt strategies opposite to neighbouring strategies. That fits the very short correlation length (2) and the large number of domains (4.5) better than true coarsening does.
