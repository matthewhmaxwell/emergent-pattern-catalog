First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** This is the Nowak–May spatial Prisoner's Dilemma with synchronous imitate-the-best updating. T=1.9 sits inside the chaotic window.

**1. Model family.** Cells on a 64×64 torus have 8 neighbours plus self-interaction. They play a two-strategy Prisoner's Dilemma with R=1, P=0, S=0 and T=b=1.9. Every cell then copies the strategy of the highest-scoring cell in its 3×3 neighbourhood. This is the spatial evolutionary Prisoner's Dilemma, also called the Nowak–May model or the spatial game with "imitate the best neighbour".

The match is exact on each point:
- Nowak and May's setup is described as follows: players play the PD with nearest neighbouring sites and with their own site, a 3x3 territory. Each site is then taken by the player with the highest score among the previous owner and the immediate neighbours.
- The payoffs match too. Following Nowak and May, S=P=0, R=1, T=b with 1<b<2.
- The Nowak–May analysis gives a 2x2 or larger D cluster that keeps growing at its corners for b>1.8, and shrinking for b<1.8. Together with the C-cluster condition for b<2, this gives the chaotic window of roughly 1.8<b<2.

- **Earliest source:** Nowak, M.A. & May, R.M., "Evolutionary games and spatial chaos", *Nature* 359, 826–829 (1992). The search confirms the citation details, and the paper was published 29 October 1992. The long-form follow-up is Nowak & May (1993), "The spatial dilemmas of evolution", *Int. J.
