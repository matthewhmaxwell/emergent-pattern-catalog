**What the numbers show.** Only one type is left (n_types = 1.0), the largest domain covers the whole lattice (largest_domain = 1.0), activity falls steadily (activity_trend = −4.22), and there is one reversal. In short, the system ran until one strategy took over everything, which is an absorbing state. It doesn't look like a textbook coarsening picture because the coarsening is already finished. A frozen, uniform end state cannot have the usual growing-domain-size curve. That explains the mismatch with "textbook examples" without needing anything new.

**Why each ingredient is already documented:**
- **The game step is the Nowak–May model.** Players use "imitate-the-best": each copies the action of her neighbor who earned the largest payoff, including herself. The R=1, P=0, S=0, T=b member is exactly the Nowak–May parameterization. Without mutation, all-cooperate and all-defect are known absorbing states: runs continue until the density of cooperators in the lattice reaches an asymptotic average value or else it becomes 0 or 1. A replication of Nowak–May on a small (13×13) lattice found that the asymptotic level of cooperation is either 0 or a large value close to 1, depending on the initial condition.
