First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** I found no twist in the observed behaviour that the literature doesn't already cover. What looks unusual is how the run got tagged as "spatial game chaos", which is probably a side effect of the added randomness rather than anything new.

**1. The first pass was right that the main game isn't a Prisoner's Dilemma.** The payoffs T=1.5, R=1, S=0.5, P=0 put S above P, so it's a snowdrift (Hawk-Dove) game. In the standard benefit/cost form, when one cooperates and the other defects, the latter receives T = b while the former is penalized with the total cost S = b - c. That gives b=1.5, c=1, so the cost-to-benefit ratio c/(2b−c) is 0.5. Hauert & Doebeli covered this case in their Nature paper: spatial structure reduces the proportion of cooperators for a wide range of parameters, and eliminates cooperation if the cost-to-benefit ratio is high. In spatial snowdrift, cooperators form small filament-like clusters and lack the ability to give rise to compact clusters on lattice. That fits what was measured: correlation length 2 and about 4.6 small minority patches.

**2. The g=(1.3, 0) sibling is outside the chaotic regime.** That sibling is the exact Nowak-May game. On the 8-neighbour-plus-self lattice, their chaotic regime is 1.8 < T < 2.0, where the frequency of cooperators converges to 0.3178.
