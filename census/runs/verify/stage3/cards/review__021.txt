First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** This is not documented as a new form of spatial game chaos, and the "chaos" label is probably an artifact of the noise. The first pass was right about the noise but too generous in calling it a variant. Nothing in what was observed needs a new mechanism.

**1. The game is a snowdrift game, not a Prisoner's Dilemma.** With T=1.5 > R=1 > S=0.5 > P=0, the payoffs are in the snowdrift (hawk–dove) order. Hauert & Doebeli showed that in unstructured snowdrift games, intermediate levels of cooperation persist. Unexpectedly, spatial structure reduces the proportion of cooperators for a wide range of parameters. They also describe the spatial patterns: while in the spatial prisoner's dilemma cooperators can survive by forming large, compact clusters, in the spatial snowdrift game cooperators form only small filament-like clusters. That is, it is advantageous to adopt strategies that are opposite to neighboring strategies. Small, broken-up clusters fit the short correlation length (2) and the several small domains (about 5). Also, the well-mixed snowdrift equilibrium is (S−P)/[(S−P)+(T−R)] = 0.5/1.0 = **0.5 cooperators**. That would give a type entropy close to 1, which matches the observed 0.989. My reading is that the system behaves like a randomly mixed snowdrift population sitting at its textbook coexistence point.

**2. The mixing steps are a documented ingredient.** Copying a random neighbour every step (a voter-model step) or swapping with a neighbour at p=1.
