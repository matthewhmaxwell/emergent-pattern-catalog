**What the rule is.** This is a cyclic cellular automaton (CCA) with 4 states, the 8-cell neighbourhood and a threshold of 1. The only change is that cells don't all update every step: each one that is able to advance does so with probability 0.3. The basic rule is the textbook one: each cell remains unchanged until some neighboring cell has a modular value exactly one unit larger than that of the cell itself, at which point it copies its neighbor's value.

**The random version is documented.**
- Bramson and Griffeath studied random-update cyclic systems first. The CCA was proposed by Bramson and Griffeath as the deterministic counterpart of the cyclic particle system, in which vertices update their colors asynchronously. Firing with probability 0.3 per step is a discrete-time stand-in for that.
- The behaviour seen here, noisy wave fronts rather than clean spirals, is described directly in Fisch, Gravner and Griffeath (1991). Their abstract says the deterministic rules show nucleation of locally periodic 'ram's horns' or spirals, while corresponding random processes give rise to a variety of 'forest fire' equilibria that display large-scale stochastic wave fronts.
