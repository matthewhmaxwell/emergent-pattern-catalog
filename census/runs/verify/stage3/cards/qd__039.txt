**Why the PD program ends up there:**
- **The base game is textbook.** In Nowak and May's model, the agents are greedy and imitate indiscriminately the action of the wealthiest neighbor. They used T=b (where 1<b<2), R=1, P=0, and S=0. Your S=−0.5 makes this a strict prisoner's dilemma, which is harsher on cooperators than their version.
- **Without the extra rule, the end states are already known.** Full defection and full cooperation are absorbing states of the dynamics because there are no mutations. Also, if the initial number of cooperators is small, they cannot form clusters and full defection is finally established.
- **The second rule only pushes one way.** Cooperators with at least 4 defector neighbours turn into defectors, and nothing ever turns a defector back. That steadily breaks up cooperator clusters at their edges and drives the grid toward all-defec
