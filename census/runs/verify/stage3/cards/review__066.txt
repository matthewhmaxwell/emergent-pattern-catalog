**1. The game rule is a textbook Nowak–May model, but with snowdrift payoffs.** Each cell plays its 8 neighbours and itself, and copies the best scorer. That is exactly the 1992 setup: every agent plays the game with all his neighbors (once with each neighbor) and with himself, then adopt[s] the strategy of one of the agents in this set who has obtained the greatest payoff. Nowak and May used CD-payoff = DD-payoff = 0, CC-payoff = 1, DC-payoff = 1.85, which is a borderline Prisoner's Dilemma. Here T=1.5 > R=1 > S=0.5 > P=0. That is the snowdrift ordering, with benefit b=1.5, cost c=1 and cost-to-benefit ratio r=0.5. That case is also well documented. Hauert & Doebeli showed that spatial structure reduces the proportion of cooperators for a wide range of parameters, because in this game it pays to adopt strategies that are opposite to neighboring strategies. That anti-coordination pressure is exactly what produces many small, interleaved patches rather than growing domains. Related work covers the same ground: Killingback & Doebeli (Spatial evolutionary game theory: Hawks and Doves revisited) and Fu, Nowak & Hauert (Invasion and expansion of cooperators in lattice populations: Prisoner's dilemma vs. snowdrift games).
