First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** Confidence is medium. The behaviour is generic chemotactic aggregation of agents that climb a diffusing field. It is not a new phenomenon. The one thing I couldn't check against a source is the Gray-Scott-like, agent-tracked field in program 1.

**1. Model family**
- This is a hybrid of self-propelled agents and diffusing chemical fields. Agents steer up a gradient (chemotaxis). The fields follow reaction-diffusion with cubic autocatalysis, a rate of f·g² as in Gray-Scott.
- Program 1 (the described system): agents climb field 0, and field 1 grows autocatalytically by consuming field 0. Agents don't write to any field, so they act as tracers of a Gray-Scott-type pattern.
- Program 2 (CAF): agents attract each other within a radius and deposit into field 1. This is autochemotaxis, the Keller-Segel-type loop in which agents make the field they climb.
- The cell lattice and the 4 agent "types" appear to play no role in either rule set. Type entropy is 1.385, which is essentially ln 4 = 1.386, so the types are fully mixed overall. The type segregation of 0.254 is most likely a small-cluster composition effect, not a mechanism. I did not test this.

**2. The behaviour and the sources**
The standout features are strong clustering (density CV 2.0, nearest-neighbour ratio 0.39) and locally aligned motion (0.43). Both are the expected signature of agents converging on gradient maxima.
