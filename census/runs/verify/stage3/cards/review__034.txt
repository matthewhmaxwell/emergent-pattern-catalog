**Why it's known**

- **The main rule is a cyclic CA with a voter-style step.** A cell is triggered by any neighbour in its successor state, then copies a random neighbour instead of the successor. The textbook rule is that each cell remains unchanged until some neighboring cell has a modular value exactly one unit larger than that of the cell itself, at which point it copies its neighbor's value. Copying a random neighbour weakens that cycle push and adds voter-like smoothing. Both of those lead towards coarsening rather than lasting spirals.
- **Four states that persist while domains grow is the standard result for N=4.** For cyclic Lotka-Volterra systems, the arising spatial patterns form a mosaic of single-species domains with algebraically growing size, ℓ(t)∼t^α, where α=3/4 (1/2) and 1/3 for N=3 with sequential (parallel) dynamics and N=4. For N<5 ... single-species domains arise and grow indefinitely, and the process exhibits coarsening.
