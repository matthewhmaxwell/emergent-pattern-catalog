RULE (plain words):
World: a 64x64 lattice of cells (wrap-around edges, 8 neighbours each). Cells have 4 types (random start). Rules, applied in order each step: (1) a cell of type t becomes type t+1 (cyclically) if at least 2 neighbours already are t+1 (every step); (2) neighbouring cells swap contents (every step).

First-pass text (excerpt): **Verdict: KNOWN.** Rule (1) is the textbook case, and I couldn't find any behaviour here that isn't already described. The odd overall fingerprint is most likely because the swap step breaks up the spirals.

**Rule (1) on its own is the standard spiral case.** It is Griffeath's cyclic cellular automaton with 4 states, the 8-cell neighbourhood and threshold 2. Fisch, Gravner and Griffeath map exactly this space: T=1 and T=2 are both locally periodic, but T=2 produces spiral waves, while T=1 "quenches" incoherent local oscillations. The standard illustrations of this phase diagram use these same settings: the number of colors is fixed to 4, the rule uses a range-one box-shaped neighborhood, and the initial conditions are a uniform random mixture of colors. So this rule pairing isn't new.

**Rule (2), swapping neighbours, is the standard way to add movement in cyclic-competition models.** There are two standard ways to implement mobility in systems where at most one particle is allowed at any lattice site: diffusion, realised through the hopping of particles to empty sites, and particle swapping, where particles on neighbouring sites exchange places. The four-type cyclic case with swapping has been studied directly.
